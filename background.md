# Project background

## Static bearings
Most trackballs use static bearings: three tiny balls in a triangular pattern, with the main ball perched on them (like a tripod). The bearings are embedded in the shell and don't move at all (hence, "static").

Static bearings are silent and inexpensive, but suffer from [static friction](https://www.britannica.com/science/static-friction) ("stiction"): you have to apply enough force to dislodge the trackball when it's at rest, and when the ball finally moves it jumps too far. Stiction makes it difficult to carry out small, precise movements. It's the bane of all trackballers.

Some static trackballs suffer more than others. You can have two seemingly identical trackballs that have very different stiction, and the only way to guarantee avoiding it is to avoid the static bearings.

## Ball transfer units
A ball transfer unit (BTU) is a specialized type of bearing that contains a big ball bearing sitting on top of a bunch of smaller subbearings that can all move freely within the bearing case. This allows the big bearing to spin freely, since there's very little friction slowing it down. However, BTUs can be noisy due to the ball bearings moving and colliding inside the case. They're generally meant for commercial/industrial use, where noise isn't an issue.

You won't find BTUs in most consumer-focused devices (e.g. Logitech or Kensington), but they're popular in DIY trackballs and modifications. Bosch Rexroth produces the best BTUs, but they're difficult to buy in small quantities and expensive when you can get them (typically CAD$15-20 per unit). That's a significant barrier to entry for a lot of people.

## Put those hands together!
My semistatic design merges the static and BTU concepts. It features a main bearing that can spin freely on subbearings (like a BTU), but the subbearings are embedded and don't spin (like a static bearing). It produces very little noise due to only having one moving part, and virtually eliminates stiction since the trackball doesn't have to fight as much to dislodge itself.  And since it's 3D-printed and filled with inexpensive ball bearings, the only barrier to entry is access to a 3D printer.
